# AI Writing Tropes to Avoid

Source: [tropes.fyi](https://tropes.fyi) by [ossama.is](https://ossama.is)
Content was rephrased for compliance with licensing restrictions.

When generating prose, blog posts, documentation, commit messages, or any written content, avoid the following common AI writing patterns. A single instance may be fine — the problem is when multiple tropes stack up or one repeats throughout a piece. Write like a human: varied, imperfect, specific.

---

## Word Choice

- Don't overuse "quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally", "remarkably", "arguably" to inject false significance.
- Avoid "delve", "certainly", "utilize", "leverage" (as verb), "robust", "streamline", "harness".
- Don't reach for ornate nouns like "tapestry", "landscape", "paradigm", "synergy", "ecosystem" where simpler words work.
- Don't replace "is/are" with "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents" — just use the simple copula.

## Sentence Structure

- Avoid the "It's not X — it's Y" reframe pattern. One per piece max, not ten.
- Avoid the dramatic countdown: "Not X. Not Y. Just Z."
- Don't self-pose rhetorical questions and immediately answer them: "The result? Devastating."
- Don't repeat the same sentence opening multiple times in succession (anaphora abuse).
- Don't overuse rule-of-three (tricolon) patterns, especially back-to-back.
- Drop filler transitions: "It's worth noting", "Importantly", "Interestingly", "Notably".
- Don't tack on shallow "-ing" analysis phrases: "highlighting its importance", "reflecting broader trends".
- Don't use "from X to Y" constructions where X and Y aren't on any real spectrum.

## Paragraph Structure

- Avoid excessive short punchy fragments as standalone paragraphs for manufactured emphasis.
- Don't disguise listicles as prose by wrapping numbered points in "The first... The second... The third..." paragraphs.

## Tone

- Skip false suspense: "Here's the kicker", "Here's the thing", "Here's where it gets interesting".
- Don't default to patronizing analogies: "Think of it as...", "It's like a...".
- Avoid futurist invitations: "Imagine a world where...".
- Don't simulate vulnerability or self-awareness performatively.
- Don't assert something is obvious instead of proving it: "The truth is simple", "History is clear".
- Don't inflate stakes to world-historical significance for mundane topics.
- Skip the pedagogical hand-holding: "Let's break this down", "Let's unpack this", "Let's explore".
- Don't attribute claims to unnamed "experts", "observers", or "industry reports" — name sources or don't cite them.
- Don't invent compound concept labels ("supervision paradox", "acceleration trap") and use them as if they're established terms.

## Formatting

- Limit em dash usage. 2-3 per piece is human; 20+ is AI slop.
- Don't start every bullet with a bolded keyword phrase.
- Avoid unicode arrows (→) and smart/curly quotes — use standard keyboard characters.

## Composition

- Don't summarize at every level (fractal summaries). Say it once.
- Don't beat a single metaphor to death across an entire piece.
- Don't stack historical analogies rapid-fire to build false authority (especially in technical writing).
- Don't restate one argument ten different ways across thousands of words (one-point dilution).
- Don't repeat sections or paragraphs verbatim.
- Don't announce conclusions with "In conclusion", "To sum up", "In summary" — the reader can feel the ending.
- Don't follow the rigid "Despite its challenges..." formula where problems are acknowledged only to be immediately dismissed.
